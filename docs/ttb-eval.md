# Accuracy on real applications

Generated by `scripts/eval_ttb.py report` from `samples/ttb/eval_results.json` and the hand review in `samples/ttb/fail_review.json`.

## What was tested

The test set is 120 approved applications drawn at random from TTB's Public COLA Registry: 40 spirits, 40 wines and 40 malt beverages, approved between November 2023 and October 2026. Each was checked with all of its label images and the production settings (claude-haiku-5-5, claude-opus-5-5, 15 second limit), 4 checks at a time.

TTB's application record gives only the brand name and, for imports, the country of origin. So those two fields and the government warning were checked. Class/type, alcohol content, net contents and the bottler were not evaluated.

Every one of these labels was approved. A Fail on one is either a checker mistake or a problem TTB let through, so each one was opened and classified by hand.

Needs review is the most common result. Among labels that need review, the findings that most often need it are Brand name (40); GOVERNMENT WARNING: in bold (20); Rest of the warning not bold (20); Country of origin (13). Of the 44 brand names that need review, 16 are labels that lead with another name and print the brand elsewhere, 15 show only part of the name on record or add words to it ("perennial" for PERENNIAL ARTISAN ALES), and 11 are names the two readings disagreed on. The bold and paragraph findings need review whenever the two readings disagree or can't tell, which is common on small back-label print.

6 of 120 were marked as images that can't be reliably assessed. 0 checks returned no result. In 3 checks only one of the two readings came back in time.

## Fails on approved labels

In the first run, 34 of 120 approved labels got at least one Fail, with 36 Fail findings in all. Every label was opened. 26 were checker bugs, 0 were reading errors (the label says something else), and 10 were genuine label issues.

The checker bugs had four causes, all fixed with tests:

- Labels often set the warning tight, as in "(1)According" or "defects.(2)". Spacing is now ignored, like line breaks. A hyphen that splits a word at a line break is ignored too.
- Labels print accents that TTB's records leave out, as in Martí and MARTI. Accents are now ignored.
- Many labels lead with a product or brewery name and print the brand smaller, or use the bottler's name as the brand. The reading kept only one name, so the brand was never compared. The reading now also lists the label's other names. When the brand appears in one of them or in the bottler statement, the result is needs review instead of fail.
- The same words in another order, or run together in a logo, now need review instead of failing.

## Changed brand names

30 copies had their brand name replaced with another record's brand. None of these should pass.

- 30 of 30 did not pass overall after the fixes, and 30 of 30 before them.
- 29 of 30 failed the brand name finding. The rest went to needs review.

## Speed and cost

- Median 7.8 seconds per check, 90th percentile 10.4 seconds, slowest 15.0 seconds. 66 of 120 applications have two or more label images, and every image goes to both readings, so checks take longer than on the one-image generated labels.
- About 3.5 cents per check from the token counts at list prices. The 157 checks cost about 6 dollars.

## How the fixes were applied

The rule fixes were applied to the saved readings with `scripts/eval_ttb.py rejudge`, which makes no new calls. Only the 7 labels whose brand was printed somewhere the old reading didn't capture were checked again, after the reading started listing other names: 24268001000443, 25009001000284, 25176001000297, 25204001000531, 25364001000103, 26235001000098, 26279001000464. The other labels keep their first readings, which don't have the list of other names.
